ExposureIQ

Active research Private repo

Measuring how exposed public companies are to emerging risks, from what executives say on earnings calls.

ExposureIQ ingests a five-year panel of U.S. earnings calls and classifies each text segment against a risk theme through a cooperative multi-agent pipeline, where four specialist agents share findings on a blackboard before voting. The harder half of the project is the evaluation harness: it scores not just the system's output but each agent inside it, records enough provenance to reproduce any number, and diffs runs example-by-example — so a prompt tweak can't quietly trade good answers for bad ones.

106,597 Text segments
1,000 Earnings calls
5-stage Agent pipeline
88 Tests

The Problem

Emerging risks — integrity failures, compliance exposure, governance breakdowns — show up in executive language long before they show up in filings or headlines. Earnings calls are a dense, quarterly, cross-sector source of that language: a standing invitation to measure something that financial statements cannot see.

Two things make that hard.

The signal is multi-label and boundary-fuzzy A single answer in a Q&A session can touch accountability and honesty at once, or neither. Reasonable annotators disagree on where one component ends and the next begins — so the ground truth is itself contested.
An LLM pipeline you can't decompose can't be trusted End-to-end F1 tells you the system is wrong. It does not tell you whether the gate dropped the segment, an agent hallucinated evidence, or the synthesizer overrode a correct vote.

Plenty of work treats the first as the interesting problem. ExposureIQ treats the second as the actual engineering problem — a pipeline making up to ten model calls per input is only as useful as your ability to say which call is wrong.
