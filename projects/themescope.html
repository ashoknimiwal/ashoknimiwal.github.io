ThemeScope

Prototype Private repo

An LLM-powered research system that scores companies on structural themes from their own disclosures — where every score is traceable to the sentence that produced it.

Two of the largest risks in a modern portfolio never appear on a balance sheet: how structurally exposed a company is to an economy-wide shift like AI, and whether you can trust how its management talks about it. Both are sitting in plain sight in earnings calls and 10-K filings. ThemeScope reads them — and shows its work, sentence by sentence.

Built on the ExposureIQ research pipeline. ThemeScope is the conversational, auditable system that grew out of ExposureIQ — the same multi-agent classifier and evaluation discipline, wrapped in a query agent you can talk to.

The Problem

Both obvious ways to measure this fail — one too blunt to catch the signal, the other too unstable to defend.

Keyword counting The classical approach can't tell aspiration from execution, and can't tell an executive taking responsibility from one using the very same vocabulary to avoid it. The words match; the meaning is opposite.
Asking an LLM to "score this 0–100" Fails worse. The number is unstable across a universe, drifts silently the moment you upgrade the model, and — fatally for anyone defending it to an investment committee or a regulator — cannot be audited. You can't point at a portfolio and say the model felt that way.

ThemeScope is built on the single constraint that fixes both.

The language model is a reader and a classifier. It is never a scorer.

Agents read text and emit labels with their reasoning; the score is deterministic arithmetic over those labels, and nothing else. That one separation turns a black box into a research instrument — the output becomes reproducible, point-in-time, backtestable, auditable to the sentence, and safe to upgrade the model underneath.

Enforced as three laws

Not a guideline — three rules the system obeys in code, not in a prompt.

  1. 1

    The model classifies and orchestrates; it never emits a number that reaches the output.

  2. 2

    Scoring is offline and append-only — a prompt or model change writes new rows under a new config hash, it never overwrites an old score.

  3. 3

    Provenance rides on every row, and unverified data is excluded from every cross-sectional statistic.
